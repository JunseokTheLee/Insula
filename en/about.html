About

What is Weavo

Weavo is a collaborative art campaign. Anyone can submit a piece of art and watch it become part of something larger — every submission is placed into a shared weavo based on its dominant color, building a picture that only comes together because everyone contributed a piece.

Why we built this

The campaign centers artists with and without disabilities working side by side, with no separation between the two. Every piece counts equally toward the whole, regardless of who made it or how.

How a mosaic comes together

Every submission's dominant color is read automatically and matched to the closest open cell across every active campaign — so the full picture only comes into focus as more artists add their piece, each one staying visible at full size and credited to its artist.

Mosaic

From a single upload to a place in the whole.

  1. 01

    Submit your artwork

    Upload any medium from your profile — add a title and description if you'd like. There's no campaign to pick.

  2. 02

    Weavo reads its color

    Your piece's dominant color is analyzed automatically the moment you submit it. No tagging required.

  3. 03

    It's matched to a campaign

    It's placed into whichever open cell, across every active campaign, its color fits best. Nobody places pieces by hand, and no one chooses which campaign a piece joins.

  4. 04

    The picture comes together

    The image builds itself one submission at a time and only resolves into a whole once enough pieces have landed. Every piece stays visible at full size and credited to its artist, permanently.

It's a mosaic in reverse: a mosaicist starts with a picture in mind and cuts pieces to fit it. Weavo starts with nothing but color logic — the artwork comes first, from people who've never seen each other's work, and the image only emerges in hindsight. Step back and it reads like any mosaic. Step closer and each "tile" turns out to be someone's complete artwork.

Who's behind it

Weavo is an initiative by ABST, a Jeju Island-based organization that brings together high school art students and local autistic/disabled youth artists to build exhibitions rooted in real collaboration. Questions, feedback, or ideas — reach out at jadyenthelee@gmail.com.
